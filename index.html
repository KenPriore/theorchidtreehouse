Built in 1958

A Palmer & Krisel house in Sunrise Park, built by the George Alexander Construction Company and sold new from $19,900. Its history, and a first look at what we’re doing with it.

See the house↓
01 / THE HOUSE✳

Designed by architects, built by master builders.

In 1958 builders in Palm Springs were selling a new kind of house: open plans, low rooflines, a living room that opens to the garden through a wall of glass.

Orchid Tree is one of those houses, built by the George Alexander Construction Company in its Enchanted Homes tract, on William Krisel’s Ramon Rise plan. It is a modest house, one of well over a thousand the Alexanders built in Palm Springs.

The work ahead starts from what is original: the plan, post and beams, the roofline, the stacked stone.

THE BUILDER

George Alexander Construction Company

George Alexander and his son Robert built well over a thousand modern houses in Palm Springs between 1957 and 1965, on the conviction that an architect-designed home could fit a modest budget. Their advertisements said it in three lines: designed by architects, built by master builders, for permanent value.

THE ARCHITECT

William Krisel, AIA

Born in Shanghai in 1924, Krisel ran Palmer & Krisel with his partner Dan Saxon Palmer, the firm behind the Alexanders’ best-known tracts. His papers and drawings, the plan for this house among them, are kept at the Getty Research Institute.

THE PLAN

Ramon Rise

Krisel drew it in January 1957: three bedrooms, two baths, about 1,200 square feet. It was his second pass. The version that got built sets the kitchen beside the bathrooms, an economy that kept the plumbing runs short, and opens the living room to the garden through a wall of glass.

02 / ONE PLAN, THREE NEIGHBORHOODS

Built three times in eighteen months

Krisel’s Ramon Rise plan was built three times in eighteen months, in three parts of Palm Springs, under two brand names. Houses in all three neighborhoods share one floor plan. They are numbered here in the order the neighborhoods broke ground.

1

Sunmor

ENCHANTED HOMES · FROM 1955

East of Farrell Drive. Builder Bob Higgins broke ground on Sunmor Estates in 1955 with houses by Wexler & Harrison. The venture stalled, and around 1957 the Alexanders took up the unbuilt land. From January 1958 they sold the Ramon Rise plan here as Enchanted Homes. Some classifieds call it Enchanted Estates.

MODEL HOME
2866 Livmor Avenue

2

Little Beverly Hills

ENCHANTED VILLAGE · FROM 1956

South of Ramon Road, west of El Cielo. SonGold Land Development broke ground on Ramon Rise Estates in October 1956 and built the first twenty or so houses. The Alexanders bought the remaining 64 lots and built them out as Enchanted Village between July 1957 and February 1958. Krisel drew the Ramon Rise plan for this tract, and it was built here first.

MODEL HOME
640 Compadre Road

Sunrise Park

03 / FROM THE ARCHIVE

“For now
and forever.”

That was the promise in a March 1958 Enchanted Homes advertisement. Beside it: a national design-merit certificate naming George Alexander Co. and Palmer & Krisel.

The ad promised design that would last, in a house for everyday living. Nearly seven decades later, we are taking the ad at its word.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Same language
Present tense

Restoration would return the house to 1958 and keep it there. Renovation would treat 1958 as something to get past.

We’re after the ground between the two. Work with the Ramon Rise plan and keep what makes it a Krisel house: post and beams, the long line of the roof, the stacked stone at the front, the western exposure and clerestory glass that open the living room to the San Jacinto Mountains. Then modernize the home systems, and make each new thing as its builders might have with today’s materials.

Krisel called his work a language. We want the house to keep speaking it.
