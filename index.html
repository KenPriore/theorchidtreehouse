PALM SPRINGS, CALIFORNIAAN ALEXANDER HOME · EST. 1958

“For now
and forever”

So promised the 1958 advertisement for Enchanted Homes. Nearly seven decades later we own one, on Orchid Tree Lane in Palm Springs, and we are about to find out how well the promise holds up.

See the house↓
01 / THE HOUSE✳

Designed by architects, built by master builders

In 1958 builders in Palm Springs were selling a new kind of house: open plans, low rooflines, a living room that opens to the mountain views through a wall of glass.

Orchid Tree is one of those houses, built by the George Alexander Construction Company in its Enchanted Homes tract, on William Krisel’s Ramon Rise plan. It is a modest house, one of well over a thousand the Alexanders built in Palm Springs. Somewhere in the decades after, it drifted away from the language it was drawn in.

THE BUILDER

George Alexander Construction Company

George Alexander and his son Robert built more than a thousand modern houses in Palm Springs between 1957 and 1965. They believed a family on a modest budget could afford a house designed by an architect. Their ads put it this way: designed by architects, built by master builders, for permanent value.

THE ARCHITECT

William Krisel, AIA

William Krisel was born in Shanghai in 1924. He and his partner, Dan Saxon Palmer, ran Palmer & Krisel, the firm that designed the Alexanders’ best-known neighborhoods. His drawings and papers are kept at the Getty Research Institute, and the plan for this house is among them.

THE PLAN

Ramon Rise

Krisel drew the plan in January 1957. It has three bedrooms, two baths and about 1,200 square feet. It was his second try. The version that was built puts the kitchen next to the bathrooms, which kept the plumbing short. A wall of glass opens the living room to the mountain views.

02 / ONE PLAN, THREE NEIGHBORHOODS

Three neighborhoods that share an architect and a builder

The same floor plan went up in three neighborhoods. Sunmor, Little Beverly Hills and Sunrise Park were built by the same company, to designs by the same architects. As in many midcentury developments, one floor plan was built under several rooflines, so neighboring houses rarely matched: a flat roof, an angled one, and a double gable with one roof set slightly in front of the other. Their houses are part of the midcentury style Palm Springs is known for. They are numbered here in the order the neighborhoods broke ground.

1

Sunmor

ENCHANTED HOMES · FROM 1955

East of Farrell Drive. Builder Bob Higgins broke ground on Sunmor Estates in 1955 with houses by Wexler & Harrison. The venture stalled, and around 1957 the Alexanders took up the unbuilt land. From January 1958 they sold the Ramon Rise plan here as Enchanted Homes. Some classifieds call it Enchanted Estates.

MODEL HOME
2866 Livmor Avenue

2

Little Beverly Hills

ENCHANTED VILLAGE · FROM 1956

South of Ramon Road, west of El Cielo. SonGold Land Development broke ground on Ramon Rise Estates in October 1956 and built the first twenty or so houses. The Alexanders bought the remaining 64 lots and built them out as Enchanted Village between July 1957 and February 1958. The design had its start in Sunmor, dual gable included. Krisel set it down here as the Ramon Rise plan, and it then carried over to Sunrise Park.

MODEL HOME
640 Compadre Road

3

Sunrise Park

ENCHANTED HOMES 3 · 1958

West of Farrell Drive. In May 1958 the Desert Sun announced “a new group of houses which will extend east from Sunset Way” to join the houses already standing across Farrell. The Enchanted Homes here are concentrated on three consecutive blocks. The rest of Sunrise Park has its own history, including Jack Meiselman’s Palm Lane Estates and the work of other builders.

03 / FROM THE ARCHIVE

The advertisement, March 1958

“For now and forever” ran across an Enchanted Homes advertisement in the Desert Sun. Beside it: a national design-merit certificate naming George Alexander Co. and Palmer & Krisel.

The ad promised design that would last, in a house for everyday living. Nearly seven decades later, we are taking the ad at its word.

Enchanted Homes 3 completed

2015

Enchanted Homes 3 named in the city survey

The city’s historic resources survey identifies the Enchanted Homes, this house among them, as contributors to a potential Sunmor / Enchanted Homes historic district, and to the midcentury character and legacy of Palm Springs.

2017

A neighboring house is nearly demolished

One block away, a 1958 Alexander house was set for demolition. Neighbors from both sides of Farrell Drive turned out, the preservation board voted unanimously for a stay, and the house was saved and restored.

“…something that should be saved.”William Krisel, on midcentury Palm Springs

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Same language
Present tense

Restoration would return the house to 1958 and keep it there. Renovation would treat 1958 as something to get past.

Our plan sits between them. We start with the view. The western exposure and the clerestory glass open the living room to the San Jacinto Mountains, and we will protect that view before anything else. Around it we work with the Ramon Rise plan and keep the post and beams, the long line of the roof and the stacked stone at the front.

We’ll update the systems behind the walls and make more room to breathe, including space for both of us to work from home. The new work will take its cues from the original house.

When it’s finished, we want you to walk in and feel that it all belongs together. We’ll use today’s materials in ways the original builders might have recognized, carrying the same care through every detail, from where a run of tile stops to how a bank of cabinets meets the wall.

Krisel called his work a language, and we want the house to keep speaking it long after this work is done.

KRISEL BEFORE PALM SPRINGS

Woodland Hills, 1954

Four years before Orchid Tree, Palmer & Krisel designed Corbin Palms in Woodland Hills for the Alexander Construction Company. The ideas that later shaped their Palm Springs tracts were tried there first, at neighborhood scale: post-and-beam construction, vaulted wood ceilings, clerestory glass and open plans.
